01 / BUSINESS CONTEXT

The problem.

For a lending business, understanding past defaults can help prioritize risk monitoring and reveal patterns worth investigating. The goal is to analyze associations—not to claim a validated credit-scoring model.

  1. 01 What is the overall loan default rate, and how does it vary by credit score?
  2. 02 How does debt-to-income ratio relate to observed defaults?
  3. 03 Which loan purposes show higher default rates?
  4. 04 How do employment status and years employed relate to defaults?
02 / WORKFLOW

From two tables to clear insights.

Joined borrower-level characteristics to application-level loan outcomes using borrower_id. Confirmed row counts, checked unique identifiers and unmatched records, then segmented borrowers and measured default rates using a consistent denominator.

04 / TECHNICAL SKILL

Validating the KPI with SQL.

A straightforward aggregate counts loan applications and measures the fraction with a binary default flag. The exact same KPI can be reproduced with Pandas for cross-checking.

loan_default_mysql.sql · MySQL 8+
SELECT
    COUNT(*) AS total_loans,
    SUM(defaulted) AS total_defaults,
    ROUND(AVG(defaulted) * 100, 2)
        AS default_rate_pct
FROM loan_applications;
05 / DECISION SUPPORT

What I would recommend investigating.

Future analysis should test whether the observed relationships hold on a larger, representative dataset. Rather than applying a loan-approval cutoff from these sample rates, I would recommend evaluating different cohorts, data quality, temporal stability and fairness before considering any operational policy.

Important limitations: This is descriptive analysis on generated data, not causal inference, a production credit model, or a basis for approving or denying loans. Interpret segments with their sample counts and verify potential confounders.
