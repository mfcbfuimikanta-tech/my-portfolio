01 / BUSINESS CONTEXT

What drives sales?

An online beauty and personal-care retailer wants a clear view of completed sales, best-performing products, geographic performance, and returning customers.

  1. 01 How much completed revenue did the business generate?
  2. 02 Which products and cities contributed the most?
  3. 03 How did revenue change throughout the year?
  4. 04 How many customers completed repeat purchases?
02 / DATA MODEL

Four tables, one revenue definition.

Joined customers → orders → order_items → products through their shared identifiers. Used the item-level transaction price rather than the current catalog price so the recorded value represents each order.

04 / SQL METHODS

Reproducible metrics.

This query joins order headers to line items, excludes incomplete orders, and aggregates line-level sales revenue. The full script also covers monthly trends, products, cities, average order value, repeat customers, and integrity checks.

MySQL 8+ · Completed revenue
SELECT ROUND(SUM(oi.quantity * oi.unit_price), 2)
       AS completed_revenue
FROM orders AS o
JOIN order_items AS oi
  ON o.order_id = oi.order_id
WHERE o.order_status = 'Completed';
05 / RECOMMENDATIONS & LIMITATIONS

What I would investigate next.

Compare the leading products across months, review why cancelled and returned orders are not completed, and investigate whether Mukalla's performance persists in a larger representative sample. Validate revenue against refunds and discounts before adopting a financial reporting definition.

Limitations: Synthetic demonstration data, only 120 orders, and simplified order status/price treatment. Figures demonstrate SQL analysis rather than evidence of real customer behavior.
